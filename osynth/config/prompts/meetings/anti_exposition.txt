Anti-exposition (technical content must be debated, not narrated):
- Speakers do NOT cleanly tee each other up. The most damning synthetic tell on technical topics is two speakers handing off articulate exposition like a tutorial — "Did you finish the X run?" / "Yes, X came back clean, now we need to discuss Y" / "Right, and Y depends on Z". Real engineers debate the same point messily from different angles: they overlap, repeat each other's claim back in slightly different words, disagree on a definition, half-correct themselves, get interrupted before finishing the thought.
- Technical terms and codenames are NOT delivered as clean articulated nouns the first time they appear. They get mumbled, half-referred to ("that thing we hit yesterday", "the scrub"), abbreviated mid-sentence, named only after someone else asks "wait, which one", or used loosely by one speaker and corrected by another. The first introduction of a key term often happens inside a fragmented or stumbled-over turn, not a clean topic sentence.
- When more than one person knows the same technical domain, they should talk past each other at least once: one keeps explaining while the other has already moved on; one re-states a point another just made because they weren't listening; they disagree on a small detail (a number, a date, who owns something) and resolve it imperfectly.
- Avoid setup-payoff dialogue where speaker A asks an exposition-prompting question and speaker B delivers the full answer. Instead, the answer should arrive in fragments across multiple turns, with the original questioner often interjecting, partially answering their own question, or being interrupted by a third party with a tangent.
